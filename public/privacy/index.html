This notice covers the Proof website, waitlist, signed-in web app, and Proof connector used through ChatGPT or another supported assistant. The assistant platform has its own privacy policy for your conversations and its handling of connector results.

Data Proof handles

  • Account identifiers and display names. WorkOS handles hosted sign-in and receives authentication information such as your email address. Local username accounts store a salted password hash.
  • Draft text, document titles and versions, uploaded files, source metadata, extracted pages and passages, reference entries, and search requests.
  • Check inputs, findings, evidence excerpts, reports, task progress, provider usage, and estimated processing costs linked to your workspace.
  • Connector account identifiers, platform/client identifiers, permission scopes, selected source identifiers, import requests, and tool requests.
  • Waitlist email address, signup time, signup placement, and your permission to receive early-access updates.
  • Technical request information used to operate the service and limit abuse. The waitlist uses your IP address for rate limiting.
  • Information you choose to include in support emails.

Documents may contain personal information about other people. Only submit material you have permission to process. Avoid passwords, credentials, sensitive personal records, and confidential material that you cannot share with the providers described below.

Why Proof uses data

Proof uses account and workspace data to authenticate you, store your work, extract source text, retrieve evidence, run the checks you request, return results, and manage connector permissions. It uses technical and usage records to keep the service working, limit abuse, and measure processing costs. Waitlist details are used for early-access updates; support messages are used to answer your request.

Proposed legal bases for operator approval: performance of the service contract for requested account and analysis services; legitimate interests in security, abuse prevention, operational reliability, and handling support; consent for waitlist emails. A provider-processing checkbox records your choice about that operation and does not by itself establish a GDPR legal basis for every item of personal data in an uploaded document.

Connecting and disconnecting an assistant

Proof links connector access to a Proof account and workspace. It records the platform, authorized permissions, and selected sources. The connector receives only the results of the tools it calls under those permissions. This does not give Proof access to your entire assistant conversation history.

You can revoke a platform grant in Proof's integrations workspace. Revocation stops further authorized access and cancels associated checks. Imports already underway may finish. Revocation does not delete your stored sources, previous work, or copies already returned to an assistant platform.

Storage and retention

Persistent workspace records live in a database. Original source files live in private object storage. Account records and Railway waitlist entries also use persistent server-side files. Earlier class and evidence workflows keep some jobs and results temporarily in server memory; those expiry periods do not delete persistent Studio records.

Your browser stores drafts, workspace preferences, icons, and onboarding state in local storage. Clearing site data removes that browser copy. It does not delete server-side work. Hosted authentication uses necessary session cookies, including a session cookie with a seven-day lifetime and a short-lived login-flow cookie. Expiry or signing out does not delete your account.

Proposed retention criteria: keep account and saved workspace content while needed to provide the active account and saved-work service, then remove it when an authenticated deletion request is fulfilled. Keep waitlist details while early-access updates remain relevant and permission remains active; remove them when permission is withdrawn. Keep support and security records only as needed to resolve the issue, prevent abuse, or meet applicable legal duties. The code currently has no automatic inactive-account or waitlist purge. The operator must approve these criteria and establish deletion procedures. Backup, log, queue, and provider retention remain to be verified.

Deletion and privacy rights

You can delete individual library sources in supported workspaces. Proof removes related extraction and check records and queues removal of the stored source file. Cleanup can wait for an outstanding signed upload link to expire and for the worker to run. Archiving a document hides it from the active list and keeps it stored.

To request account deletion, access, correction, export, or withdrawal of waitlist permission, email the support address above. Include the account email or identifier and the request; do not send your password or access token. Proof has no self-service account deletion button.

Where applicable law grants them, you have rights to access, correct, erase, restrict, object to processing, and obtain a portable copy of your data. Withdrawing consent does not affect processing that was lawful before withdrawal. You can complain to your local supervisory authority, including the Czech Office for Personal Data Protection.

Operator approval required: accept and establish the manual account-deletion and rights-request process, including identity verification, account and workspace removal, authentication-provider handling, and backup limitations. No fixed completion deadline is promised in this draft.

Automated results and updates

Proof generates evidence assessments using automated retrieval and model judgments. Results can be incomplete or wrong. Proof's checks are intended for human review, not decisions about a person's legal rights or similarly significant decisions.
